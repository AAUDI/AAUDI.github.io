01 · Freelance · Embedded AI · UAV

Real-time visual tracking on a Jetson for FPV drones

The operator picks one target; the drone keeps it locked while both move. A state-of-the-art tracker adapted for the Jetson with a 30 fps low-latency link. The clips: a real test flight through the drone’s own camera, then benchmark runs on UAV123 and DTB70.

30 fps at 1280×740 · Jetson Orin Nano Super

02 · Lynred · Infrared · Embedded AI

An AI denoiser for thermal cameras, small enough to live in the camera

Raw thermal video is grainy and striped. The embedded denoising model I worked on at Lynred cleans each frame on the camera’s own processor and keeps the edges; Lynred presented it at SPIE DCS 2025 as Lynred Denoiser Net. Drag the divider.

Runs in the camera · Proc. SPIE 13469

03 · Lynred · Infrared · Image quality

Image quality for thermal sensors, from the raw pixels up

Raw infrared images are uneven, striped and grainy, with dead pixels. At Lynred I worked on the chain that cleans them (non-uniformity correction, defective pixels, noise and stripes, contrast and detail), then detection and tracking, in C++ on a PC and on the camera’s SoC.

Raw sensor → clean video · PC and in-camera C++

03Services

Three ways to start.

Pick the one that matches where your project is. Each ends with something you can hold, and each can stop there.

  1. 01

    Feasibility & camera choice

    Before anyone buys hardware or labels a frame: can a camera see this at all, and which one?

    • Sensor and optics recommendation sized to range, resolution, light and budget
    • A data-collection and labelling plan you can run yourself
    • The risk list, with the one experiment that settles each risk
    • A written report you can hand to a supplier or an investor

    2 to 3 weeksFixed deliverable

  2. 02

    Prototype sprint

    A working pipeline on your data, end to end, so the decision to invest is made on evidence.

    • Acquisition, the model or the classical method, and the decision logic
    • Evaluation on held-out footage, with the numbers written down
    • A demo you can run without me, and the code in your repository from day one
    • A clear note on what it would take to make it a product

    4 to 8 weeksMilestones

  3. 03

    Productisation & edge port

    The prototype becomes something you ship: on a server, in a box, or on a 10-watt board.

    • C/C++ implementation, tests and non-regression benches
    • Streaming, recording, GUI, alerts: the application around the model
    • Deployment on Jetson, GPU, SoC or FPGA with the power and latency budget met
    • Hand-over: documentation, and your team able to maintain it

    Scoped per projectTime & materials

Pricing and the engagement model are quoted after a short call, once the scope is clear. Remote by default; on site in France or Lebanon when the hardware needs it.

04What I build

I build computer vision end to end: the camera and the data, the algorithms and models, and the application that ships them, wherever it has to run.

A vision project is rarely just a model. It starts with the sensor and the optics, goes through data collection and labelling, classical image processing and deep learning, calibration and 3D, and ends in software someone actually operates: a pipeline, a service, a GUI, an alert. I have done each of those steps for clients, and most projects needed several of them at once.

When the application has to run on the edge, that is a skill I have in depth: a Ph.D. at IGN putting a real-time stabilisation algorithm inside a drone camera's FPGA, a postdoc at CEA porting tomography to GPUs, four years on Lynred's image-signal-processing team making infrared cameras see better, a year of space electronics at Airbus Defence and Space, and today the lead on embedded vision R&D at Oreyeon. But the chip is the last step of a project, not its subject. I prototype in Python, write the product in C/C++, and move to CUDA, OpenCL or VHDL only when the budget asks for it.

I have also taught since 2014, part-time and alongside the engineering: today computer vision, deep learning and parallel computing at Saint Joseph University of Beirut and the Lebanese American University; before that C, C++, systems and architecture at ESIEE and ESIEA in Paris. Explaining a method to a room is still the best test of whether I understand it.

Away from the desk: hiking, biking and photography.
