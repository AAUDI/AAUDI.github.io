01 · Sense

From the project · Pick a view

Day job · Vision R&D · 2023–now

Vision R&D at Oreyeon: the Mobile Detection Unit, from sensors to edge SoC

Oreyeon’s Mobile Detection Unit rides on an airport’s own patrol vehicle and inspects the pavement as it drives: foreign object debris and pavement damage are flagged, classified, sized and geolocated as the vehicle passes, down to objects under 2 cm, by day and by night, with no internet connection. I joined in April 2023 as senior lead computer vision engineer and lead the vision work, fully remote. New algorithms are researched in Matlab and Python, then written in C/C++ and deployed on AI-ready systems-on-chip with CUDA and TensorRT. Topics so far: integrating and fusing the unit’s sensors (cameras, IMU, GPS), online camera calibration, stitching, feature matching, object detection and tracking.

My part
Sensor integration and fusion (cameras, IMU, GPS), algorithm design and the C/C++ implementation, test environments for validation and non-regression, validation in real conditions, and the technical lead of the vision work

03 · Batch

From the project · Pick a view

Freelance · Video analytics · Jetson

A multi-camera pipeline on DeepStream 7, now with a local vision-language model

One generic pipeline takes RTSP cameras, batches them on the GPU of a Jetson Orin Nano Super, runs a TensorRT detector and emits events. Cameras can be added or removed and the model swapped while it runs; rolling recording, snapshots and a debug view sit beside the inference without slowing it. In 2026 the pipeline gained a scene-analysis stage: selected frames go to a small vision-language model served locally by llama.cpp, which answers short questions about the scene. Nine models were tried inside the 8 GB budget; six would not even load until the switch to memory-mapped GGUF weights. The evaluation also showed the limits honestly: these models describe what they see, not why, and a staged theft on a kitchen camera read as ordinary work to all five models tested.

My part
Architecture and implementation of the pipeline and its Python interface, the test suite and the feature-impact benchmark; then the VLM integration and its evaluation: memory, latency, prompt design, concurrency
Fact to check
4 streams: 87 detections/s at 25 % CPU and 873 MB RSS, recording costs under 9 % of throughput. A yes/no question is answered in 0.3 s (SmolVLM2-500M) to 3 s (Cosmos-Reason2-2B); a full description takes 50 to 60 s
Model choice
SmolVLM2 for alerts (four cameras in parallel in about 3 s), Cosmos-Reason2-2B for periodic scene analysis, Qwen3-VL-2B for detailed reports

04 · Detect

From the project · Pick a view

Freelance · Thermal · Embedded AI

A thermal object detector, from camera choice to live alerts

A real-time detector for outdoor thermal video, built from nothing: choosing the LWIR camera for the range, field of view, resolution and sensitivity required; the camera-to-processor acquisition pipeline; data collection and labelling guidelines; a custom YOLO model; and a multithreaded C++ inference pipeline with boxes and alert logic. The clips are handheld LWIR recordings from the field at dusk. On the close ones, a generic YOLO11 detector with BoT-SORT boxes each deer and tracks it through the camera motion; on the hard cases, deer half hidden in tall grass or small at the edge of the brush, it finds nothing, which is why the project trains its own model, and a CSRT tracker started on the deer holds it instead.

07 · Pick

Freelance · Robotics · 3D vision

Vision-guided bin picking and assembly, without fixtures

A robot picks sheet-metal parts from a bin and assembles an enclosure (body, cover, two clips) from camera input alone. The closed loop runs in Isaac Sim with a UR10e: a depth-only pick proposal, a four-channel YOLO segmentation on RGB-D, a depth shape fit that identifies the part, FoundationPose with the fitted mesh, tracking of what is already assembled, and cuRobo for the motion. Ground truth is used only to grade. The pipeline was then put through 17 appearance perturbations (low light, glare, haze, blur, sensor noise, white-balance drift and more): pick targets stayed sub-millimetre, the place side flipped by 180° on the symmetric cover in 11 cases until an anchoring rule fixed all of them, and perturbation-augmented training hardened the segmentation.

My part
The perception pipeline (segmentation, shape fit, pose, grasp points), the symmetry handling and the pick-order logic
Status
Works in simulation under all 17 perturbations, with a ZED 2 depth-noise model in the loop; now moving to real scenes
Fact to check
Place flips 11/17 → 0 with anchoring; pose error 0.4 to 3 mm over 9 picks × 18 conditions
  • Isaac Sim
  • YOLO RGB-D
  • FoundationPose
  • cuRobo
  • 6-DoF pose
  • Python
08 · Measure

From the project · Pick a view

Freelance · Industrial · C++

Perception pipeline for an industrial 3D time-of-flight camera

A smart camera that measures objects on a production line in 3D and gives a pass/fail answer by itself. The sensor is a 320 × 240 time-of-flight imager (850 nm laser illumination, 0.3 to 7.5 m, 37° × 28° field of view, up to 20 fps) and everything runs inside the camera: from the raw depth and amplitude images to a filtered point cloud, a taught background, segmentation of the objects in user-drawn regions, object statistics (length, width, height, orientation), contour alignment and pose. The embedded functions ship as three use cases (distance measurement in up to 64 regions, completeness checks of compartmentalised packaging, and object sizing to about ±5 mm), configured without code and exposed over IO-Link, digital outputs and a REST API. Two years of C++ on that pipeline, up to a product launched in November 2025.

My part
Image-processing and computer-vision pipeline in C++ over two years: depth and amplitude handling, point clouds, segmentation and mask refinement, auto-thresholding, contour alignment scoring, object statistics
Fact to check
Public datasheet: 320 × 240 ToF, 300 to 7500 mm, ±5 mm, three embedded use cases (distance, completeness, sizing), REST API; demonstrated at a trade fair in November 2024, launched November 2025

12 · Stack

From the project · Pick a view

Embedded vision · FPGA · Ph.D.

IMU-aided image stacking in a drone camera

A drone camera that needs a long exposure takes ten short ones at 30 fps instead. FAST corners are found in the first frame, one per block of a 10 × 10 grid; the gyroscopes predict where each one lands in the others, so ZNCC matching only searches an 11 × 11 window; least squares gives the rotation (or a homography) between frames, with outliers dropped; every frame is resampled into the first one’s geometry, exactly at block corners and bilinearly inside, and stacked into one sharp, low-noise image, inside the camera. The surveys were flown on a Copter 1B helicopter UAV from Wikhydro.

My part
Algorithm, FPGA design in VHDL and HLS, and the ARM software, on a Xilinx Zynq 7030 behind a 20 MP CMOS sensor
Fact to check
Feature detection 1.64 s → 0.02 s and resampling 36.9 s → 0.41 s once moved into the FPGA. MDPI Sensors 2017 ↗ · UAV-g 2017 ↗

13 · Reconstruct

From the project · Pick a view

HPC · GPU · Postdoc

Tomographic reconstruction on CPUs and GPUs with Kokkos

Two imaging algorithms from CEA’s CIVA software for non-destructive testing: X-ray 3D reconstruction (FDK) and ultrasound imaging (TFM), used to find and size defects in parts. I rewrote them in C++ with Kokkos so a single source runs fast on both multicore CPUs and GPUs. In FDK, every projection is weighted and ramp-filtered row by row, then back-projected into the voxel grid; each voxel only reads the filtered projections, so the back-projection is one parallel loop over the voxels that Kokkos maps to OpenMP threads on a CPU and to CUDA threads on a GPU. The animation shows the method on a small cast part with pores and a crack.

My part
Porting and optimising both algorithms in C++/Kokkos, and evaluating the library across CPU and GPU architectures
Fact to check
Presented at ParCo 2019, Prague

  • Kokkos

  • OpenMP

An AI denoiser for thermal cameras, small enough to live in the camera

Raw thermal video is grainy and striped: temporal noise, fixed-pattern residue, soft edges. On Lynred’s image-signal-processing team I worked on the embedded AI denoising model that became Lynred Denoiser Net (LDN): a compact network that cleans each frame on the camera’s own processor, keeping edges, faint warm details and the natural look of the scene. Lynred presented it at SPIE Defense + Commercial Sensing 2025 alongside its toolbox.

15 · Correct

From the project · Pick a view

Lynred · 2019–2023 · Infrared · Image quality

Image quality for thermal sensors, from the raw pixels up

Straight off an infrared sensor, the image is barely usable: each pixel answers the same scene a little differently and that difference drifts, a few pixels are dead or blink, row and column stripes and temporal grain sit on top of the scene, and the raw range is too wide for a display. On Lynred’s image-signal-processing team I worked on the steps that turn it into a clean picture: non-uniformity correction, defective-pixel detection and replacement, temporal-noise and stripe removal, and contrast and detail enhancement, then detection and tracking on the corrected video. Every algorithm ran in C++ both on a laptop and on the embedded SoC inside the camera.

My part
The algorithms for each step of the chain, their C++ versions for the PC and for the camera’s embedded SoC, and detection and tracking on the corrected video, as part of the ISP R&D team
Fact to check
Corrected, shutterless video straight out of the camera, in Lynred’s public video “Infrared images with ATI modules for UAVs”

  • Defective pixels
